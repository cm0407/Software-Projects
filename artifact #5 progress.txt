Care Org Tracking App: Brainstorm Summary
Nov 15, 2026 · @Abdulrahman Elmi
Project overview
We're building a web app that replaces manual, paper-based logging at care organizations: attendance, daily activities and each person's needs. It starts as a personal learning project, with the option to become a business later.
The idea came from seeing childcare operators we know log activities and attendance by hand.
Market landscape
Basic check-in/out, profiles and parent messaging are table stakes, so they won't make us unique on their own.
• Childcare: a crowded, mature market led by Procare and Brightwheel, and still consolidating (Procare acquired Playground in September 2026). Source
• Senior care / adult day: tools like StoriiCare already cover attendance, care plans, medication records, family messaging and billing. Source
• The gap: smaller programs remain underserved. A Procare industry report found 44% of programs without software still use paper and pencil. Source
Target users and approach
Childcare organizations are our first users. Senior homes come next, and a third org type is still open (candidates: after-school programs, adult disability day programs, respite care, community centers).
At signup, a user picks their organization type and the app tailors its experience to that type. How we structure this underneath is our first architecture decision, still to be made.
Key childcare pain points: strict staff-to-child ratios, verifying authorized pickups, constant hands-busy logging (naps, meals, diapers, incidents), subsidy billing, and high staff turnover.
Chosen differentiating features
All three target the same root problem: staff are busy with kids, and logging gets in the way. The pitch: log it in seconds, and the app notices what you might miss.

Known tensions and open decisions
• Voice vs. offline: most good speech-to-text runs in the cloud. Options: queue audio until back online, use a weaker on-device recognizer, or make voice online-only.
• Alerts need history: pattern alerts only work after weeks of clean logged data, so they come after logging is in use.
• Observations, not diagnoses: alerts say "nap times dropped this week," never "possible developmental issue."
• Audio privacy: recordings include children's names and details. Decide whether audio is deleted right after transcription and how we tell families.
• Org-type architecture: separate apps per type, one shared app with a type setting that toggles features, or a shared core with pluggable modules per type. Not decided yet.
Draft roadmap
A starting sketch, open to reordering.
1.  Phase 1 (core): org signup with type selection (childcare only at first), child profiles with allergies and authorized pickups, classrooms, staff accounts, kiosk check-in/out, and manual activity logging. Option: build the kiosk offline-first from day one, since retrofitting it later is harder.
2.  Phase 2: voice logging on top of the activity log, plus a simple daily summary for parents.
3.  Phase 3: pattern-change alerts once real data exists, starting with simple rules (attendance drops, meal and nap trends).
